## Proposal
Using data from the New York Police Department (NYPD), we are studying enforcement patterns in New York City by matching cases in NYPD Complaint Data to cases in NYPD Arrest Data. Our classification model will identify which reported cases lead to a follow-up arrest versus those that do not. By analyzing features such as demographic information on the alleged perpetrator (race, sex, and age), crime type, and location, we can see how these factors are associated with enforcement activity (or a lack thereof). Given the NYPD’s massive budget, finding evidence of an enforcement gap would suggest that systemic bias, as opposed to underfunding, drives the selection logic for law enforcement of different populations. The findings would increase transparency on the NYPD’s selective enforcement actions and highlight any discrepancies in such actions across precincts to interrogate institutional
accountability. Machine learning enables us to analyze historical complaint and arrest data to identify if certain communities are systematically targeted by enforcement and to detect patterns of discretionary bias across precincts. This could also provide predictive insights on future enforcement actions, accounting for demographic and precinct-level factors as drivers of systemic outcomes.
